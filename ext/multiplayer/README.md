# Multiplayer

Up to eight players in one game (fewer if the host sets `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_MAX_PLAYERS`): one hosts, opening their game and their save to the others, who join it by address and port.
Everyone plays the host's level together and makes progress on the host's save.

- The host is Mario. The others are Luigi, Wario, Metal Mario, Fire Mario, Purple Mario, Orange Mario and Ice Mario, in the order they join; each sees the others, and themselves, as their character.
- A player who customized Mario in the launcher (Customize Mario) wears their own colours instead of their character's, in every game: each game sends its player's colours to the host, which passes everyone's on to everyone, players who join later included. Each game paints the others itself, in HD when it has the HD textures, from Mario's pictures the launcher prepares (`SMS_MARIO_PAINT`); without them, everyone keeps their character's colours.
- A player's name shows over their head while you are near them, fading out as they get farther away. Players are called by the names they joined with ("Player 2" without one) everywhere, in the notices and the player list too, not by their characters'.
- Shines, blue coins, red coins, coins and lives are shared: what anyone collects counts for everyone, and the host's save keeps it. Every 50 coins of the shared count is one more life for everyone (the host's game gives it, once, whoever's coin it was), with the 1-up's sound in every game.
- Coins and items one player takes disappear for everyone, including those that appear during the level (from a box, a defeated enemy or a switch): those are matched by type and place, and one that appears in a game after someone took theirs is gone as it appears, so nobody collects one twice.
- A switch pressed in any game (a red coin switch, a switch that throws out hidden coins) is pressed in every game, without taking the others' controls or camera.
- When the shared coin count passes 100, the 100-coin Shine comes out in every game, beside each player, as it does for the player whose coin makes exactly 100.
- Enemies are shared: each is run by the game of the player nearest it (when near, else the host's), so it chases, grabs and lets go of that player as it would alone, and everyone else sees it where that game has it. An enemy defeated in any game is defeated in all (and comes back when it comes back for the host).
- While the host's game is paused, the others' worlds (enemies, NPCs, critters, machines, bosses) run on their own, and follow the host's again once it plays.
- Cutscenes are watched together: when one starts in any game (a switch's camera, an event's), everyone is taken to the player who started it and their game plays the same camera. Not the level's opening (every game plays its own), a gate's camera (it follows the gate), or a shared boss's own cameras (its fall, its Shine coming out: every game's copy plays its own when the host's does). A camera that only shows something coming out (a blue coin or coins from a sprayed graffiti, a Shine appearing, what a switch did) is watched by everyone from where they are, nobody taken to it; nor is anyone taken to where there is no floor. A Shine coming out comes out in every game with its camera, not when that game's own event gets there (then its camera is not played again).
- Bosses are shared (`game/mp_boss.cpp`): the host's boss is the real one, every other game's copy takes each step of the host's fight when the host's does and follows its position and the state of the fight, and a hit landed in any game counts on the real one. When it goes down, it goes down in every game and its Shine comes out for everyone. Petey Piranha (water in his mouth, ground pounds on his belly), Gooper Blooper (eyes, beak, his ink and attack mode, and each tentacle: what it does, its shape, stomped flat or torn off; tearing one off in his first fight takes everyone on, as it takes the player who did), Eely-Mouth (each tooth's dirt), Phantamanta (every piece: its size, hits and place), Wiggler (its hit points and which segment can be hit), King Boo (spicy or not, fruit hits), Mecha-Bowser (plates and their hits; each player rides their own coaster), Bowser's hot tub (each corner, broken in every game; the last starts the ending everywhere) and Shadow Mario's chases (where he is, his water meter, stomps).
- Water sprayed by any player is sprayed in every game: it washes goop away and hits enemies everywhere, and cleans a graffiti as much as it cleaned theirs. A graffiti erased in any game is erased in every game, what it hid out (a blue coin, coins, a Shine), and comes back in every game when that goes back in it untaken; a player who joins, or loads the stage again, finds each as it is now. A graffiti whose blue coin someone else got is gone, as when the stage loads.
- What a player carries (a fruit, a barrel) is carried in every game, and put down or thrown where they put it down or threw it.
- One level at a time, the host's. A player who joins goes straight to it, with no file to choose. Anyone leads the group to another area: a way out taken by any player (a pipe, a painting, a door, a secret course's entrance, a story event's) takes everyone, with its movie, so the fades go together (another player's is asked of the host, who sets off with everyone). A player's own lost life, Shine or pause menu Exit Area are theirs alone: they come back to the host's level.
- The host chooses the episode. When the group heads into a world with an episode select, every game goes to its own, where the others watch the host choose: their controller does nothing there, their cursor follows the host's, and the host's choice is made for them.
- A Shine touched by anyone is got by everyone: every player is taken beside the one who touched it, in a line with them in the middle holding it, and their game plays the whole Shine Get (the camera, the pose, "SHINE!", the fanfare and the fade); the host then saves (the prompt, or Auto Save), and the others wait a few seconds for it and go on with it: to the plaza, or wherever the host's mods send everyone (the world's next episode, with Seamless Episodes); a host still saving after that is waited for in the plaza. The other prompts to save (a blue coin, a nozzle box opened for good, Yoshi hatched) are the host's too: a joining player's own is skipped, and the host's game asks once its player is on the ground and nothing else is on.
- Moving to another level fades out and in for everyone, as the game's own trips do, with the new level's opening.
- Falling out of the world (through a floor, with no floor at all beneath for a second and a half) is a lost life, as into a pit, not a fall for good.
- Losing a life plays the usual death, "TOO BAD!" and its jingle, then puts that player back at the start of the same level, alive and keeping the coins, instead of sending them to Delfino Plaza. Lives are shared, so every player's deaths count against them.
- With no lives left, a death shows the game's GAME OVER, but the player is not sent to the title: they come back to the level watching the others, the camera riding with one player at a time (L or R, or left and right on the D-pad, to watch another). A 1-up brings players back: each player watching takes one of the shared lives as it comes and appears beside the player they watched (lower slots first). When every player is watching (out of lives), everyone leaves the world for Delfino Plaza with three lives (in the plaza, it starts again), as soon as the last player's game over is over; when the host moves on to another level, everyone goes along, alive.
- Players see each other's position, facing, animation (pumping FLUDD and the nozzle in use included), FLUDD's aim (the nozzle, the waist and the head turning with it, as Mario's do), cap, hands, Yoshi, the Blooper they surf on, the coaster car they ride through Mecha-Bowser's fight, and their effects (a slide's spray, a landing's dust, the steps' splashes). Players near yours are heard too: their steps, jumps, landings and ground pounds, FLUDD's rattle and their voice, a little quieter than your own Mario and fading out with distance (gone past about 25 metres). A player who is not the host sees themselves the same way, aim and coaster car included.
- Objects run by one game and seen in the others (lifts, blocks and clouds on rails, spinning blocks, windmills) are drawn smoothly a moment behind that game, as players are. Noki Bay's mud boats and Bianco Hills' leaf boats are every player's own, so one rider can't leave the others stranded: the others see a copy of the rider's boat under them, moving with them. Every secret course's moving pieces are shared: its rail blocks, lifts and clouds, spinning blocks, the sand bird (it flies where the host's does, smoothly), and sand blocks (one crumbling under any player crumbles for everyone). The plaza's manhole lids are shared too: a player ground pounding into one (or jumping up into it from below) flips it open in every game, with its sound.
- Save states are off while playing together.
- The game is the host's: when the host leaves it (closes their game, or goes back to the title with Save & Quit or the reset), everyone else's game says so and goes back to its title screen, out of the multiplayer game. A host that goes silent without leaving (a crash, a lost connection) counts as gone after the timeout (`SMS_MULTIPLAYER_TIMEOUT`, 10 seconds); the others' games try to reach it again for 6 seconds more, then leave the same way.

It is peer to peer: no server, the joining games talk straight to the host's.
The host must let the others reach that port: forward it on their router (UDP, 24002 unless another is chosen) to their computer.

## Starting it

SMS Launcher has a **Multiplayer** button beside Settings: **Host Game** starts the game hosting, **Join** first checks that a game answers at the address and port, then starts the game to join it.
Without the launcher, these variables do the same:

| Variable | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER=host` | host, on `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_PORT` (24002 by default) |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER=join` | join `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_ADDRESS` (`host`, `host:port`, `[ipv6]:port`) |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_NAME=name` | the name the others see (23 characters) |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_MAX_PLAYERS=n` | host: the most players the game takes, the host included (2 to 8, default 8); more are refused with a message saying so |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_TIMEOUT=s` | seconds of silence before a player counts as gone (default 10) |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_STATUS=0` | no player list in the corner (the notices still show) |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_NAMES=0` | no names over the other players |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_COLLISION=1` | host: players collide with each other (the settings menu's **Player Collision**, on the Gameplay page while hosting, changes it as the game goes on) |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_LOOK=hat=2fa846;shirt=2fa846` | this player's colours, by Customize Mario's region names (the launcher sets it) |
| `SMS_MARIO_PAINT=dir` | Mario's pictures and their colour regions, for painting the others in their colours (the launcher's `bakePaint` makes it): `paint.txt` and a `.paint` file per texture, described in [`sync/mp_look.cpp`](sync/mp_look.cpp) |

A joining player's game keeps its own memory card out of it: the launcher gives it a `multiplayer-guest` card folder, since what it plays is the host's save. Back at the title with no host to follow (the host left, or refused it), its file select shows that card's files, so a notice says why the host's game ended for it, that these are not the player's own saves, and how to get to those.
By default both games must be the same build (the launcher's game version); a different one is refused with a message saying so. A host may let versions drift instead, and ask for at least some versions:

| Variable | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_VERSIONS=drift` | host: take any build of the same protocol and the same Eclipse setting (`exact`, the default: its own build only) |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_MIN_VERSION=v` | host: and at least game version `v` ("2026.10.18.1"; a game that does not say its version is refused) |
| `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_MIN_LAUNCHER=v` | host: and at least launcher `v` |
| `SMS_GAME_VERSION`, `SMS_LAUNCHER_VERSION` | this game's version and the launcher that started it (the launcher sets both); without the first, the build's own (`-DSMS_GAME_VERSION`, else its last commit's date) |

The protocol number must always match. A joining game sends its version, its launcher's and the mod API it carries; the host refuses one that is too old, saying which version it needs, and also refuses games older than its server mods' `client_api` (ext/modapi). Probes carry the host's version and what it takes, after the mode and server byte, so the launcher's join check says so before starting the game. Versions compare a number at a time (`mp_version_compare`), a beta (`1.2.0-beta`) before its release.

## Servers and server mods

A host can be a **server** rather than a player (`SMS_MULTIPLAYER_DEDICATED=1`): its own Mario is out of play and unseen, it is not counted among the players (probes say so, so the launcher lists the players without it), and the first player to join plays Mario, the next Luigi, and so on. A server needs no file: from the title it goes straight to its stage, the one a server mod asks for, else `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_STAGE=area,episode`, else Delfino Plaza, with a new save's flags. It runs headless: `SMS_HEADLESS=1 SMS_AUDIO=0 SMS_TEXTURE_PACKS=0 SMS_MULTIPLAYER=host SMS_MULTIPLAYER_DEDICATED=1 ./sms GMSE01.iso` (about 600 MB of memory).

A **server mod** (a native or Lua mod whose `mod.ini` says `side = server`, [`ext/modapi`](../modapi/README.md)) runs only in a hosting game, and runs a game mode there: the Squid Race is one ([sms-mod-templates](https://github.com/chasem-dev/sms-mod-templates/tree/main/examples/squid-race)). Players join with the plain game, nothing installed, as a Minecraft server's plugins need nothing of the players: the server mod decides when and where, and every game carries out its word with the game's own models, sounds and HUD. Its word is a **server command** (`MPG_CMD_*` in [`mp_game_api.h`](mp_game_api.h), `SMS_CMD_*` in the mod SDK), sent to any players (the host's own game is slot 0) as the reliable message `K_CMD` and carried out by [`game/mp_server.cpp`](game/mp_server.cpp):

| Command | In the player's game |
| --- | --- |
| `BANNER` | big text in the middle, in a colour (`""` hides it) |
| `NOTICE`, `STATUS` | a line of news at the top; a standing line shown when there is no news |
| `TIMER` | the HUD's clock: hidden, stopped at a time, counting up or down from it (it counts on by itself) |
| `READY_GO` | the game's "Ready... Go!", "Go!" at a moment given (each game starts it as early as its frame rate needs) |
| `SOUND`, `MUSIC` | a system sound; background music (or the command's music stopped) |
| `WARP`, `HOLD`, `MOUNT` | the player put somewhere facing a way; their controls held (a Blooper ridden stops); onto a Blooper of their own game at a place |
| `WATCH` | the player out of play, the camera riding with another player (as a player out of lives watches), or back in play |
| `ACTORS` | the stage's actors of a type range shown, hidden (out of the stage, as event scripts take them away) or killed (a Shine) |
| `STOP_SCRIPT` | the stage's event script from a resource stopped (a course's time trial, a story event) |
| `STAY` | the player's own ways out (pipes, doors, Exit Area) lead nowhere: only the host moves them on |
| `OBJECT` | one of the stage's objects (by type, kind and index, in the order every game loads them; `"@id"` a spawned one), or each of a kind: placed and turned, its collision with it; a spinner or a lift on its rail at a speed of its own; moved on from how the stage loaded it to a moment given, the same in every game (stage setup keeps the movers' first state); shown or hidden; sized, turned every way, gliding to a place a tick at a time; killed |
| `SPAWN` | a copy of one of the stage's objects, made again from its record in `scene.bin` (kept as the loader read it: the `zzzzz-ext-20` patch on `TNameRef::genObject`) at a new place, its manager's array grown for it and its hit group given it; or an item by name (`TItemManager`), only one the stage has the model of. Its id names it after ([`game/mp_spawn.cpp`](game/mp_spawn.cpp)) |
| `PLAYER` | the player: the game's own miss (`loserExec`), thrown (`throwMario`), off a Blooper or Yoshi, health, FLUDD's water (a level, or a share more), how fast FLUDD uses it, a nozzle, FLUDD on their back or off it |
| `RULES` | no FLUDD (R held off), no damage (the `playerDamage` hook), no pause (Start and Z held off), no pickups (the stage's items out of reach while it holds), water hits players, no refills (the `fluddRefill` hook) |
| `BOARD`, `HUD`, `MARKER` | rows of text at the left (the overlay's board panel); the HUD's parts hidden each frame; text or an arrow on the picture over a place or a player (the overlay's markers, projected through the game camera) |
| `SCREEN`, `SHAKE`, `RUMBLE` | a colour over the window fading out, in, or flashing; the camera's eye and aim shaken (the `camera` hook); the controller's rumble |
| `SPLASH` | a player splashed as Shadow Mario is when FLUDD's water hits him ([below](#players-against-each-other)) |
| `BOUNDS` | an arena's edge: the player kept within a radius of a point, drawn as a line on the ground; gliding to a new radius over a time ([below](#players-against-each-other)) |

A command carries four whole numbers, eight others (the last four after its text on the wire, which older games stop before) and a text. A command may carry a **delay**, carried out that long after it was sent at the same moment in every game (each takes off half its round trip to the host): a race's start lines up across the players. A **sticky** command is kept for the stage session and replayed to each player as they arrive in it (a newcomer, a player back after falling in); it replaces the last of its kind to the same players (a session keeps up to 192). A command's effects last for the session it came in: a new session (the host moving everyone on) starts each game afresh. The host also tells the players whether it is a server and its game mode's name (`K_SERVER`, the first word to a newcomer), and answers probes with them (after the protocol number: the mode, and a byte saying it is a server).

On the host, the mod API's `api->net` (ext/modapi) sends the commands (`mpg_server_send`), asks for a stage afresh for everyone (`mpg_server_load_stage`: a new session, even for the stage played now; a server at the title goes there, a hosting player after the file select), names the mode and a line of its state for the launcher's list (`mpg_server_mode`, `mpg_server_status`), forces a player's colours (`mpg_server_set_look`: the host relays the forced look as `K_LOOK` in place of the player's own, which it keeps for when the forced one goes, and a game takes a look for its own slot too, repainting its own Mario), sets where a player comes back as they arrive (`mpg_server_set_respawn`: a `WARP` or `MOUNT` sent after the sticky replay), reads what each player presses (their poses end with their controller, after what older games read), and hears who joined, left and arrived in the session, and each new session (`mpg_next_net_event`, the mods' `SMS_EV_NET_*`). Where each player is comes from their updates (`mpg_pose`), and the host's own stage has the course's cubes (the mod API's `game->in_cube`), so a server mod judges a race itself.

## Players against each other

[`game/mp_pvp.cpp`](game/mp_pvp.cpp): players colliding, and water fights.

**Collision.** The host decides whether players collide: its settings menu's **Player Collision** (on the Gameplay page while it hosts; started from the launcher, the change is saved as `mpCollision`), `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_COLLISION=1`, or a server mod over either (`net->set_collision`, `mpg_server_set_collision`; -1 gives the host's own back). It travels in `K_SERVER` (a byte after the mode's name: bit 0, which older games do not read), sent again to everyone when it changes. Each game then keeps its own player out of the others after every movement tick, as the game keeps Mario out of an enemy he walks into (`TMario::keepDistance`: running into one fast bumps him back, `JUMP_SHORT_BACK_DOWN`), and lands him on a head as on an enemy's (`TMario::trampleExec`: the puppet takes the stomp, `TMpPuppet::receiveMessage`, and Mario bounces off with the stomp's dust and sound). Each player is moved by their own game only, so two players pressing into each other are kept apart from both sides. Riders of Bloopers pass through.

**Water fights.** With a server mod's rule `RULES` `WATER_HITS` (`MPG_RULE_WATER_HITS`), FLUDD's water hits the other players. Each game judges its own player's water: a FLUDD drop in flight (`TModelWaterManager`'s particles, state 1, flag `0x40`) inside another player's body (55 units round, 170 tall, as Shadow Mario's is about) stops there, and the hits are gathered up for a tenth of a second and sent to the host (`mpg_splash`, `K_SPLASH`: the session, the player hit, the drops, where the last one hit), which hands them to its server mods (`MPG_NET_SPLASH`, `SMS_EV_NET_PLAYER_SPLASHED`). The water a game draws for another player (`MpEmitWater`) carries that player's slot in its flags (`0x0F00`, beside the drawn-water bit `0x8000`), so every game stops its copy of a drop at the player it reaches, and never at the one who sprayed it. At close range a held spray lands about a hundred drops a second; movement runs 120 ticks a second whatever the frame rate, so the count does not change with it.

`SPLASH` shows a hit as Shadow Mario's water hit shows on him (`TEnemyMario::hitWater`): his splash (`PARTICLE_MS_ENM_WATHIT`) and hit sound (`MSD_SE_EN_COMMON_W_HIT_OK`) where the water hit; the player's models flushed with the damage fog (`SMS_AddDamageFogEffect`, Mario's damage colour) for a moment after the last hit (0.8 seconds, or the command's `f[3]`: Shadow Mario's lasts 4, but he has one attacker, and in a water fight a long flush leaves everyone pink); and his water meter (`drawHPMeter`, the blue bar over the head) for 20, his 600. A puppet fogs its own models; this game's own Mario, when the game draws him, is fogged after his own entry, once a frame by the first puppet in the players' group, and the meters are drawn there too, in the translucent pass. `DOWN` throws the player's own game's Mario back from the water, onto his back (`MARIO_STATUS_THROWN_DOWN`), as Shadow Mario falls when his meter is empty.

**Arenas.** `BOUNDS` (`MPG_CMD_BOUNDS`) keeps each game's own player within a radius of a point, across the ground: after every movement tick, one past the edge goes back to it (30 units inside, along the way to the middle, not into a wall: `TMario::checkWallPlane`), the speed that took them out taken away, so they slide along it as along a wall. Blooper riders are left alone. With `RING` the edge is drawn as a band 32 units wide along the ground and the water, a little above it, in the command's colour (red by default): its points are found on the stage's collision (`TMap::checkGround`, each from a little above the last, so it follows the ground and stays under what overhangs it) whenever the edge moves. `f[4]` seconds glide the edge from where it was to the new radius, on each game's clock: a closing arena in one command.

**Water as ammunition.** `RULES` `NO_REFILL` stops FLUDD filling up by itself (the `fluddRefill` hook, patch `zzzzz-ext-26`: the water around, bottles, fruit and 1-ups, a barrel's hip drop); the server hands water out with `PLAYER` `WATER` (a level) and `ADD_WATER` (a share more). A bottle the server spawns (`SPAWN` `bottle_large`, where the stage has bottles) keeps its look, sparkle and sound when taken, but not its refill: the server gives what it likes for it. `PLAYER` `FLUDD` takes FLUDD off the player's back (a waiting lobby) or puts it on. `PLAYER` `WATER_USE` makes FLUDD use water faster or slower: each tick, what the tank lost since the last one (not the server's own changes) is taken again in that proportion. Spraying without stopping, a full tank lasts about a hundred seconds at FLUDD's own rate.

The [Splash Battle](https://github.com/chasem-dev/sms-mod-templates/tree/main/examples/splash-battle) server mod is a water fight built on these: a meter per player, the HUD's health showing it, a closing zone, last one standing wins.

Test aids (`SMS_MULTIPLAYER_TEST`, seconds into each stage): `pvphunt@S[:T]` stands this game's player 250 units from the nearest other player (or slot T) facing them every two seconds, spraying all the while; `pvpoverlap@S` puts them inside the nearest player and logs how far apart they are for a second and a half; `pvpstomp@S` drops them onto the nearest player's head; `pvpout@S` puts them 500 units beyond the arena's edge and logs how far from its middle they are for a second and a half; `pvppickup@S` puts them on the nearest marker over a place every three seconds (a server mod's pickups); `pvpbot@S` plays by the controller as a person would (runs about a lobby without FLUDD; with it, runs at the nearest player, aims and sprays, runs round them to a new angle, jumps now and then, runs for the server's spawned pickups when its tank runs low, back in from the arena's edge: footage that moves like play); `pvpsea@S` puts them in the nearest water and sprays for five seconds (with `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_DEBUG`, the tank is logged every second). With `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_DEBUG=1` each game logs the drops its water landed (`water: N drops on ...`), each splash, each stomp and each knock-down.

## How it works

The extension has three parts, kept apart so the networking can be tested, and replaced, without the game:

| Folder | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| [`net/`](net/mp_net.h) | the network: one UDP socket, players' slots, a reliable ordered channel, the host relaying each player's state to the others. A thread of its own keeps everyone connected through loading screens. Knows nothing of the game. |
| [`sync/`](sync/mp_controller.cpp) | the rules ([`mp_game_api.h`](mp_game_api.h)): stage sessions, the shared flags' merge ([`mp_flags.h`](sync/mp_flags.h)), items taken, players' looks as packets, and painting each player in their own colours ([`mp_look.cpp`](sync/mp_look.cpp)). Knows the game only as numbers. |
| [`game/`](game/mp_game.cpp) | the game's side, built as game code: reads and writes `TFlagManager`, removes items, warps, and draws the other players ([`mp_puppet.cpp`](game/mp_puppet.cpp)). |

**Packets.** Every packet starts `SMMP`, the protocol version and a type: `PROBE`/`INFO` (the launcher's check), `CONNECT`/`CHALLENGE`/`ACCEPT`/`REJECT` (joining), `DATA` about 30 times a second both ways (the newest player states, and every reliable message the other side has not acknowledged, resent every 150 ms until it is), and `BYE` (sent three times as a game leaves or closes, so the others need not wait for the timeout). Reliable messages that do not fit beside the states and the world snapshot go in packets of their own, so a big one (the whole save, for a player joining a well-played file) never waits.

**Packets from strangers.** The host's port is open to the internet, so:
- a `PROBE` shorter than 200 bytes gets no answer, and an `INFO` is always shorter, so nobody can use the host to send someone else more than they sent;
- a slot goes only to a `CONNECT` that echoes the cookie the host sent to its address in a `CHALLENGE` (a keyed hash of the address, the player's number and the time), so a packet with a forged sender address cannot take a slot, kick a player or make the host send to someone else;
- the network thread reads at most 64 packets a pass, without the lock the game thread needs, so a flood does not hold up the host's frames; a player whose messages the game cannot keep up with, or who sends far ahead of what has arrived, is dropped or ignored;
- every value a player reports into the shared save stays within what the game can hold (coins and best coins 0 to 999, red coins 0 to 99, lives -1 to 99), so a changed game cannot write huge numbers into the host's save. It can still claim Shines it did not get: the host trusts its friends' games.
A client sends the host its own state; the host sends each client everyone else's.

**The save.** The host's flags are the truth. Each frame a client reports what changed in its own game since the last frame; the host merges that into its game and sends everyone the new values. How a flag merges depends on what it is: a Shine, blue coin, nozzle or story flag, once anyone has it, everyone has it; coins and red coins add everyone's pickups, but a client's own reset (losing a life) is not passed on; lives add up gains and losses; a world's best coin count keeps the highest. Until the host answers, a client keeps its own pickups on top of the host's values, so a coin never visibly comes and goes. The Shine and blue coin totals are counted from their flags.

**Stages.** Each time the host starts playing a stage it opens a stage session and sends it, with its flags, to everyone. A client in another session loads the host's stage with the host's flags first, so it loads as the host's did (collected blue coins and Shines are gone). Items are named by their index among the item manager's objects, the same in every game that loaded the same stage; the host keeps the session's list of items taken, and a player who reloads the stage gets it again. A graffiti erased or back (each game numbering each graffiti's changes, odd erased, even back) goes in the same list, so the newest says how each is.

**Enemies.** The stage's ordinary enemies (`TSmallEnemy`; bosses keep their own) are named by their manager's place in the conductor's list and their index there. Each update the host sends a share of them, where each is and whether it is up, through the session's world snapshot (unreliable, like players' states; every enemy goes round several times a second). After every movement tick a client eases its enemies to the host's positions and facing, defeats the ones the host's game has defeated (with their death if it just happened there, quietly if its own spawner had brought one out the host's had not), and brings back, with the game's own respawn (`resetSRTV`), the ones that are back on the host. A client's own defeats go to the host as reliable messages, and the host defeats them there.

**Deaths.** Losing a life runs the game's own miss sequence; the `missNextArea` hook sends the player back into the same stage (the same session: the stage's taken items and red coins stay as they were). Lives are a shared flag that can reach -1, used up; a death then takes the `takeGameOver` hook's way: the game over plays, the stage reloads, and the player is out (their state says so): Mario is out of play (`MARIO_FLAG_IS_PERFORMING`, no collision) and is moved each tick to the player watched, whom the camera then follows. A player watching says so through the stage's reload too (their state while loading carries it), and the host remembers who was out in this session. When every player in the game is out, the host leaves for Delfino Plaza with everyone (in the plaza, it loads it again as a new session), or, when its own game over is the last, its reload is that trip; the lives are set to 3 as they arrive.

**Water.** While a player sprays, their state carries where FLUDD's water leaves, its direction and the trigger's pressure; every other game emits the same nozzle's water there, through the water manager as FLUDD does, so it cleans and hits there too.

**Smooth movement.** Poses are stamped with the sender's game time and every one that arrives is kept. Each game draws the others a little in the past, about two and a half of their updates behind their newest pose, between the two poses either side of that moment: position and facing (the turn interpolated as a quaternion), animation frames (across a loop's end), blends, FLUDD's and Yoshi's frames. The moment drawn moves on by a steady amount each frame, the other game's time per frame of ours measured over many frames (their time and our frames each summed, so poses the host relays, which come unevenly, are measured as evenly as its own), and leans gently toward where it should be, so others move evenly even when updates arrive in bursts, frames vary in length, or the two games run at different speeds.

**Players' looks.** Each player sends their body's root matrix, its animation, frame and blend, the upper body's (pumping), the face's texture pattern, the hands and shapes shown, the cap, FLUDD's animation and nozzle, and Yoshi's animation and colour. The others' games draw a puppet in the stage's player group, beside Mario, under his light: Mario's own model with his texture recoloured for Luigi (green), Wario (yellow, with Mario's blue overalls), Metal Mario (silver), and the Marios whose red and blue are swapped for others: Fire (white, red overalls), Purple (purple, dark overalls), Orange (orange, brown overalls) and Ice (light blue, white overalls), each from its own copy of the files. What Mario's joint callbacks add to the animation (the head's look, the waist's bend and the nozzle's tilt as FLUDD aims, the hover nozzles' tilt) goes with the pose, and the puppet's own joints take it. A player who is not Mario sees themselves the same way: the game leaves out Mario's own models and draws theirs.

**Effects.** The effects Mario's own move asks of his particle manager go with the pose: those asked for every frame while they last (a slide's spray), as of the player's last tick, and the newest few asked for once (a landing's dust), numbered, so each is shown once although poses are lost or not sent. Each is where it was from the player's root, or bound to the same joint of the body or FLUDD, so the others' games show it at the puppet, following it.

**Sounds.** The sound effects Mario's own move starts (the `sound` hook) go with the pose the same way, the newest few numbered: only those the game starts once (bit 0x800 of the id) and are his, FLUDD's or a voice (categories 0, 1 and 7), near him; those started every frame while they last (a slide's scrape) stay in the player's own game. The others' games play each at the puppet, its place following them, with the voice on one handle per player so a new line cuts the last as Mario's does. They are at 70% of the game's volume, all of it within 5 metres of the listening player's Mario, fading to nothing at 25 (the sound system's own fall-off stops at a floor, as Mario's sounds are made to be heard at the camera). They go after what the player presses, so older games read the pose without them.

## Testing

```sh
make -C ext/multiplayer/net/tests run     # a host and clients as separate processes, a third of all packets dropped
make -C ext/multiplayer/sync/tests run    # the shared flags' merge rules
make -C ext/multiplayer/tools             # mp_bot, a stand-in player
python3 ext/multiplayer/tools/mp_shots.py --disc GMSE01.iso [--client] [--bots 3] [--turn]
```

`mp_bot` joins a host through the same controller the game uses, follows it from stage to stage, and stands beside the host doing what the host's Mario does (`--yoshi N` rides Yoshi, `--spray` pumps FLUDD, `--coins N` reports coins picked up, `--circle R` walks round a circle at an even speed: a smooth source, to measure how smoothly the others draw it, the host directly and the other joining games through the host; `drawn smoothness` lines with `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_DEBUG=1`), so one game shows every character.
`mp_shots.py` runs a hosting game headless, scripted into Delfino Plaza, with bots and optionally a second, joining game (`--client`), and writes both screens' captures to `build/multiplayer-shots/`.
`--test "take@N:K;kill@N:K;die@N:L;warp@N:area,episode;shine@N:ID;flags@N:K;lives@N:L;coins@N:K;drop@N;takedrop@N;touchshine@N;bosswater@N:K;bossdown@N;bosshit@N;setflag@N:ID"` (`SMS_MULTIPLAYER_TEST`, `--client-test` for the joining game) takes K coins, defeats K enemies, loses a life (with L lives left first, `die@N:0` to run out), leaves for another area, gets Shine ID's flag (on a client: the host then plays the Shine Get and leaves with everyone) sets K save flags after the Shines (a well-played save, whose full update is bigger than a packet), sets the lives to L (a 1-up), sets the coins to K, makes a Shine appear by the player and touches it (`touchshine@N`), sprays Petey's mouth K times (`bosswater@N:K`) or ground-pounds him down (`bossdown@N`), lands a hit on this game's copy of whatever boss is here (`bosshit@N`), sets a game flag (`setflag@N:ID`, hex, e.g. 50007 starts Phantamanta's fight), makes a coin appear at a fixed place near the plaza's start and takes the coins that appeared during the stage (these two counting frames with another player in sight, so both games do it together), N frames into each stage; `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_TRACE=1` logs each other player's drawn and newest position every frame (and every pose received), for measuring smoothness; `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_DEBUG=1` logs every flag reported and merged, some enemies' positions in both games, and every enemy defeated or brought back by the other games' word.
`SMS_MULTIPLAYER_MOVERS=1` lists, a second and eleven seconds into a stage, the classes of its objects that moved by themselves and how each is shared, and those that stood still and are each game's own (a survey of the secret courses found nothing moving left unshared). More test aids: `sandblock@N` drops this game's player onto the nearest sand block, `nearenemy@N:D` stands this game's player D units from the enemy farthest from the host (its game then runs it), `tear@N` tears off Gooper Blooper's first tentacle the game's own way (in his first fight its movie takes the stage on), `surf@N` gets on a Blooper, `route@N:x,y,z/x,y,z/...` puts the player on each point in turn, 10 frames apart (`surfroute@N:...`: N frames after getting on a Blooper, each time: round a race course), `nearboat@N` stands on the first fruit boat, `turbodoor@N` breaks down this game's first Turbo nozzle door as a dash would, `manhole@N` ground pounds into the nearest manhole lid (`manhole@N:1` stands 6 m from it instead, to watch another player's), `mantaout@N` brings Phantamanta out (its fight begun), `titlequit@N` goes back to the title as the pause menu's Save & Quit does, `quit@N` closes the game as closing its window does (on the host: the others leave its game), `rideboat@N[:S]` rides the nearest mud or leaf boat and drives it on for S seconds (10; `SMS_MULTIPLAYER_RIDE_ECHO=1` draws the copy the others see beside this game's own player), and `pickep@N:E` has the host choose episode E N frames into its episode select; `bluecoin@N` takes one of the stage's blue coins as touching it does (on a client: the host's save prompt), `void@N` drops this game's player far under the stage (a lost life a second and a half later), `tubmiddle@N` stands this game's player in the middle of Bowser's tub and logs what they stand on every 30 frames; `aim@N` sprays in place, aiming up and then down, and compares the player's own puppet's head, waist and nozzle with Mario's (a joining game), `nearfruit@N[:K]` stands the player by the nearest fruit (or the K-th) and picks it up, throwing it 180 ticks later, `graffiti@N` sprays the first graffiti hiding a blue coin till it is clean (`graffitilocal@N`: its water not sent, only its erasing), `graffiticoin@N` takes that graffiti's coin without cleaning it, and `shineout@N` brings out a Shine a hiding place hides, with its camera (each game logs that graffiti's state, and how many Shines are out, as they change); `--client-delay S` starts the joining game S seconds after `--client-after`'s line (a player who joins late), and `--realtime` paces both games by the wall clock (needed to judge smoothness: unpaced, game time outruns the network).
`--spray` has a bot pump FLUDD with water.
Each game runs under a 2.5 GB memory cap.

## Limits

- Each other player's model takes about 450 KB of the stage's memory. Every stage measured has some 44 MB to spare in the port (its arena is larger than the console's), so all eight (seven puppets, 3 MB) always fit.
- An enemy chases the player whose game runs it (the nearest); in the other games it goes where that game sends it, and still hurts the player there when it touches.
- Bosses, switches and the like are each game's own; the save's flags, the stage, items taken, enemies and water are shared.
